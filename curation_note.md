# Dataset Curation Note — Kenya Urban Flood Early-Warning Assistant

**Sourcing.** The 180-example dataset was curated from authoritative, publicly documented sources rather than generated or web-scraped without verification. Primary sources include the Kenya Meteorological Department's own Flood Bulletin page (meteo.go.ke), which describes the Daily Flood Bulletin issued by the National Flood Forecasting and Early Warning Centre for the Nzoia Basin; a KMD technical presentation on the Nzoia River Basin Flood Early Warning System detailing its monitoring infrastructure; and contemporaneous news coverage of the 2026 Kenya floods (Daily Nation, Wikipedia's "2026 Kenya floods" entry, ReliefWeb/ECHO Daily Flash, and Streamlinefeed), which supplied the real >20mm/24hr rainfall threshold, the list of 30 nationally affected counties, and the documented cause of most Nairobi flood deaths (drowning and electrocution after vehicles were swept into floodwater). Two international comparators — Bangladesh's community-based flood early-warning system and the Netherlands' "Room for the River" floodplain approach — were included as well-documented, widely-cited practices to inform the assistant's guidance on community-level warning gaps.

**Quality and safety criteria.** Every example was validated for correct three-part message structure, non-empty fields, no duplicates, and token length between 64 and 2048; all 180 examples passed with zero structural errors. The system prompt and every reference answer enforce a strict safety posture: the assistant never predicts whether or when a flood will occur at a specific location, always deferring that real-time call to official KMD bulletins, NDMA advisories, or county disaster management committees — mirroring how a clinical assistant would defer diagnosis to a licensed provider. The dataset was split 80/10/10 with a fixed seed, and the 18-example test set was sealed until final evaluation.

**Coverage gaps.** Evaluation showed the base model already handles general flood-safety reasoning reasonably well (average judge score 4.26/5 pre-tuning), since this is common-sense guidance many models have some grasp of. Fine-tuning's clearest value was on Kenya-specific factual questions the base model could not know — the single largest improvement in testing was on the KMD rainfall threshold question (judge score 1.8 to 3.8). The dataset is thinner on hyper-local guidance for specific informal settlements and on Swahili/Sheng phrasing; both are natural priorities for the next data-collection round, alongside verified county-level emergency contact numbers, which were deliberately left generic in this version pending official confirmation.
